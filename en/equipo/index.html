Who makes CubanWaterLab

Four PhD candidates — three in the exact sciences and one in social sciences — carry the project's day-to-day work, alongside the binational coordination, a network of associated researchers, and a wide group of collaborators in Cuba and Belgium.

Coordination
Dr. C. Ernesto Altshuler Álvarez
Cuban coordinator

Center for Complex Systems, University of Havana

ORCID →Google Scholar →
Bio →

Ernesto Altshuler Álvarez graduated summa cum laude from the Physics Faculty, University of Havana in 1986 (Diploma thesis on magnetic materials). He defended his PhD in 1994 (Thesis on superconducting materials).

He is professor of Physics at the Physics Faculty, University of Havana. For decades, he has done scientific research also in the fields of granular matter, dynamics of social insects, bacterial motility, porous materials and fluid dynamics. This diversity of fields has involved some bizarre experimentation[1][2][3].

Lately, he has been co-heading a Cuba-Belgian ARES project related to the creation of instruments for in situ monitoring of water quality in Cuban bays, which has little to do with his previous research experience — but perhaps with his amateur snorkeling experience.

Prof. Altshuler has been appointed as invited researcher at the Texas Center for Superconductivity (Houston, Texas, USA), the ESPCI (Paris, France), the Norwegian Academy of Sciences and Letters (Oslo, Norway), and the Rockefeller University (New York, USA) among others. His scientific activity has often been featured (see, for example[4]). He is Editor in Chief of the Cuban Journal of Physics[5], as well as a member of the editorial board of Granular Matter. Prof. Altshuler is a Fellow of the World Academy of Sciences (Trieste, Italy).

He has authored several books on science popularization and the history of science, such as Guerrilla Science: survival strategies of a Cuban physicist (Springer, 2017)[6]. His book "A Different Kind of Splash" will also be published by Springer within the next months, by the way.

Among his hobbies are guitar playing (and composition), cartoon drawing, writing short stories, caregiving of the elderly, plumbing, car fixing, tasteless cooking, and a dangerously long etcetera performed with different levels of love, audacity and mediocrity. He is also an avid science communicator, where his professional training and hobbies merge with an omnipresent (sometimes vitriolic) sense of humor[7].

He is supposed to be the propulsion force behind the most lovely statue of Albert Einstein ever made[8].

Dr. Denis Terwagne
Belgian coordinator

Université Libre de Bruxelles

ORCID →Google Scholar →
Bio → ♫

Denis Terwagne holds a PhD in Fluid Mechanics from the University of Liège (2011) and was a postdoc at MIT working on solid mechanics and mechanical instabilities. He is now Assistant Professor of Physics at the Université Libre de Bruxelles, where he directs the Frugal Lab at Fab Lab ULB, researching soft matter, elasticity of thin structures, and fluid-structure interactions.

PhD candidates (4)
Ing. Alex Manuel Rivera Rivera
PhD candidate, UH / ULB

Instrumentation, buoys, and spectrophotometry.

ORCID →Google Scholar →
Bio →

Alex M. Rivera Rivera is an Automation Engineer (CUJAE, 2023) and co-founder of ESPOLETA Tecnologías, the Cuban startup behind Vórtice, Cuba's first commercially produced weather station. He now runs two parallel PhD programs — Physics at the University of Havana and Engineering and Technology at the Université Libre de Bruxelles — both centered on environmental sensing and water-quality monitoring within CubanWaterLab, and has authored five peer-reviewed publications spanning fluid dynamics, open hardware, and robotics.

He documented his path through ULB's FabAcademy in 2024, and keeps a fuller record of his work at alexmanuelrivera.com.

MSc. Luis Abel Rodríguez de Torner
PhD candidate, UH / ULB

Wave channel.

ORCID →Google Scholar →
Bio →

Luis Abel Rodríguez de Torner holds a degree in Education, specializing in Mathematics-Physics, from the Enrique José Varona University of Pedagogical Sciences, where he also earned a diploma in Higher Education Didactics, and completed four years of Automation Engineering at Cujae. He holds a Master's in Physical Sciences from the Faculty of Physics, University of Havana, with a thesis on the application of Kalman filters to fluid-dynamics experiments. He is also a graduate of the international Fab Academy 2024 program, where he developed the project Making a Wave Flume.

He is currently pursuing a joint PhD between the University of Havana (PhD in Physical Sciences) and the Université Libre de Bruxelles (PhD in Engineering and Technology). Professionally, he spent five years as an instructor professor of Physics in the Mathematics-Physics department of his alma mater, and currently serves as assistant professor of General Physics and Electronics at the Faculty of Physics, University of Havana.

His main research lines span experimental physics, experimental fluid dynamics — with an emphasis on the design and construction of wave channels — and computational fluid dynamics using DualSPHysics.
